Do children and cats share an inflammatory response to coronavirus disease?

Select each tab to follow the project from question to result.

Imperial College London · in collaboration with LMU Munich

Cross-species comparative blood transcriptomics of human MIS-C and feline infectious peritonitis

Supervisors: Dr Aubrey Cunnington and Dr Claire Dunican · Thesis grade: Distinction · Manuscript in preparation

Two severe inflammatory diseases follow coronavirus infection in two different species. If their blood gene-expression responses agree, the cat disease can help explain the human one.

Why the design matters

Each species has its own cases and controls. I measured the disease effect inside each species first, then compared the two effects. This removes most of the difference that comes only from species.

The feline control group is small (n = 5), so I treat feline effect sizes with care.

I built the workflow in R. Select a step to see what it does.

Of 3,262 orthologue pairs that are significant in both species, 81.4% change in the same direction.

What the two diseases share, and where they differ

  • Shared Neutrophil-associated inflammatory programmes
  • Divergent Interferon-associated responses

Found with transcriptional-module and pathway analyses.

Blood is a mixture of cells

If a disease increases the number of neutrophils in blood, neutrophil genes go up even when no single cell changes its gene regulation. I examined this with CIBERSORTx cell-mixture estimates and feline clinical measurements.

What this means for the result

Part of the shared signal can come from changes in cell composition. So I report the concordance as a shared blood response, and I do not claim that it is all regulation inside cells.

MSc mini projectImperial · Dept. of Infectious Disease

PM2.5 and vape-liquid co-exposure

How do air pollution particles and vape liquid act together on lung epithelial cells?

A549 cellsRT-qPCRFlow cytometry

Compared single and combined exposures over 24 hours with RT-qPCR, Annexin V/7-AAD flow cytometry and LDH release.

IL6 rose 51.49-fold with vape alone versus 0.90-fold with co-exposure. These were single-experiment results (n = 1), so I reported them as preliminary and flagged possible LDH assay interference.
